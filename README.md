# Leaving GitHub

Notes, tips, and reminders for GitHub employees preparing to leave the company.

Policies and benefits change, and individual circumstances differ. Confirm details and deadlines with the appropriate benefits, HR, payroll, or plan provider before acting.

## Before your last day

### Save your records

- Export the personal employment records you are entitled to keep, such as reviews, compensation history, pay statements, and tax documents.
- Record identifiers and contact details you may need later, such as your employee ID and the correct contacts for payroll and benefits questions.
- Save personal work samples only when you own them or have explicit permission to retain them. Do not copy private repositories or confidential company information.

### Review your benefits

- Spend balances that expire when employment ends, such as eligible FSA or lifestyle-benefit funds.
- Confirm that all expected 401(k) contributions and employer matching deposits have arrived, and understand any vesting rules.
- Check what happens to your HSA. The account is generally yours, but you may want to compare the current provider's fees and investment options with alternatives.
- Learn when your health coverage ends and review COBRA or other coverage options before you need them.

### Use benefits that will disappear

- Spend any remaining company-store credit.
- Claim any permitted employee offers or gift codes that expire with your employment.
- Check whether you are eligible for Microsoft or other relevant alumni programs.

### Prepare for losing access

- Move personal accounts away from your work email address and phone number.
- Export personal contacts and note how to reach people you want to stay in touch with.
- Return company equipment and remove personal data from it according to company policy.
- Make a list of post-departure deadlines, including benefit elections, reimbursements, account transfers, and tax forms.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, but keep them safe to publish. Do not include internal links, private repository names, screenshots of non-public systems, confidential policies, credentials, personal data, or other information that belongs only inside GitHub.
